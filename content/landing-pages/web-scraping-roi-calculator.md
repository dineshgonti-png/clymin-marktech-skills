## The Real Cost of Building Scrapers In-House

Most teams that calculate in-house scraping costs look only at the initial build. They budget two or three weeks of developer time, stand up a basic crawler, and move on. Six months later, those same developers are spending 15–20 hours a week on maintenance — patching broken selectors, rotating proxies, debugging anti-bot failures, and rewriting pipelines every time a target site changes its structure.

A 2024 study by the [McKinsey Global Institute](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai) found that data engineering maintenance consumes 60–80% of ongoing data project time. For web scraping specifically, that ratio skews even higher because target websites change unpredictably and without notice.

The actual cost breakdown for a production-grade in-house scraping setup looks like this:

**Initial build cost:** One senior developer at $120,000/year salary spends 4–8 weeks building the scraper — $9,000–$18,000 in loaded developer time before the first data point arrives.

**Proxy infrastructure:** Residential proxy networks for anti-bot bypass cost $300–$2,000/month depending on volume. Commercial proxy services charge per GB, with costs escalating rapidly at scale.

**Maintenance tax:** The ongoing cost that destroys in-house ROI. A scraper serving 10 sources requires 10–20 hours of maintenance per month minimum — rising to 40+ hours when targets introduce significant anti-bot measures or structural changes. At $75/hour blended engineering cost, that is $9,000–$36,000 per year in hidden maintenance expense.

**Infrastructure and tooling:** Servers, cloud storage, scheduling, alerting, and monitoring for a production scraping pipeline add $200–$800/month in baseline infrastructure costs.

Total annualized cost for a medium-complexity in-house scraping setup serving 5–10 data sources: **$75,000–$180,000 per year**.

## The ROI Calculation Framework

Three inputs determine your specific build vs. buy ROI. Calculate each one for your team:

**Input 1 — Blended hourly rate:** What does your engineering time cost, fully loaded? For a mid-level data engineer in the US, this typically falls between $65–$100/hour including benefits and overhead. In India, $25–$45/hour for equivalent skill level. Use your actual blended rate.

**Input 2 — Hours per week on scraping:** Track this for one month. Include time spent on: monitoring failed runs, fixing broken scrapers, proxy rotation troubleshooting, data cleaning, format normalization, and requests from internal stakeholders. Teams that track this number are typically surprised — it runs 15–30% higher than their initial estimate.

**Input 3 — Current scraping budget:** Add proxy costs, cloud infrastructure, monitoring tools, and any third-party scraping services your team currently pays for separately.

**The calculation:**

Annual in-house cost = (Hourly rate × Weekly scraping hours × 52) + (Monthly infrastructure spend × 12)

## When to Build vs. When to Buy

The build vs. buy decision depends on two factors: technical capacity and strategic differentiation.

**Build in-house when:**
- Your team has dedicated data engineers with production scraping experience
- The scraping methodology itself is a proprietary competitive advantage
- You need proxy infrastructure as a standalone component for a custom pipeline
- Your organization's core product is data, and the extraction IP is part of your business value

**Buy a managed service when:**
- Your team's core function is analysis, pricing, or strategy — not data engineering
- Speed matters: you need data in 48 hours, not 6 weeks
- Maintenance overhead pulls engineers away from higher-value work
- You need compliance documentation (ISO 27001, SOC 2, GDPR) for enterprise vendor reviews
- Predictable, fixed costs matter more than infrastructure control

For most pricing managers, revenue teams, and business intelligence functions at e-commerce, travel, and financial services companies, the managed service model delivers better ROI from day one.
