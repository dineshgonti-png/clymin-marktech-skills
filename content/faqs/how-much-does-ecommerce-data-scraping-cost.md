## The Three Cost Scenarios for Ecommerce Data Scraping

**Managed service (Clymin)**: Starts from $100/month. Pricing scales with monitoring scope — number of competitor sites, SKU volume, and extraction frequency. All technical complexity is managed by Clymin: infrastructure, proxy management, maintenance, normalization, and delivery. Zero internal engineering required.

**DIY tools and infrastructure**: Direct tool costs of $500–$2,000/month for proxy services, scraping tools, and cloud infrastructure. Engineering labor for setup (4–8 weeks initial investment) and ongoing maintenance (60% of an engineer's time). True total cost: $2,500–$6,000/month depending on scope and engineer salary.

**In-house engineering**: Dedicated scraping engineer (or partial FTE allocation) at $120,000–$180,000/year in salary plus benefits (US market). Infrastructure and tool costs. True monthly cost: $5,000–$15,000+ for a comprehensive competitive monitoring program. The advantage is maximum flexibility and control; the cost is the highest of the three options.

## Why In-House Costs Are Higher Than Most Teams Estimate

The common mistake in evaluating in-house scraping is calculating only tool and infrastructure costs rather than true total cost of ownership. The hidden cost is engineering labor.

A senior engineer at a San Francisco ecommerce company earning $160,000/year costs approximately $13,000/month in fully-loaded cost (salary plus benefits plus overhead). If 60% of that engineer's time goes to maintaining scraping infrastructure — a reasonable estimate for teams monitoring 20+ competitor sites with regular updates — the engineering cost alone is $7,800/month.

Add infrastructure costs ($300–$500/month for proxies, servers, and monitoring tools) and you're at $8,100–$8,300/month for an in-house solution before accounting for the opportunity cost of that engineering time that could be spent on product development.

According to Forrester (2024), in-house scraping infrastructure for ecommerce competitive monitoring costs 8–12x more than managed services when engineering labor is fully accounted. The comparison that looks favorable on tool cost alone — "$500/month for a scraping tool versus $500/month for Clymin" — ignores the $4,000–$6,000/month in engineering labor required to make the tool produce reliable data.
