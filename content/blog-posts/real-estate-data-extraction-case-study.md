A leading Gulf retail conglomerate expanding its investment portfolio into real estate needed systematic property market data across multiple cities in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Qatar — and needed it without adding a team of research analysts. Clymin delivered automated property data extraction covering major real estate portals across the region, and the company's strategy team achieved a 35% efficiency improvement in market research cycles, compressing what had previously taken weeks into two-day turnarounds.

## The Challenge: Entering a New Asset Class Without Data Infrastructure

The conglomerate's core business was retail — managing one of the Gulf region's largest networks of branded retail stores, shopping center concessions, and direct-to-consumer channels. The leadership team had identified real estate as the next major investment vertical, both for portfolio diversification and to support the group's own retail expansion by controlling key locations.

The problem was structural: the retail operations team had deep data infrastructure for its core business but no systematic capability for real estate market research. Evaluating potential property acquisitions or development opportunities required understanding:

- Current asking prices and recent transaction prices by property type, size, and location
- Rental rate trends for commercial and mixed-use properties across target neighborhoods
- Developer activity including new launches, completion rates, and handover pipelines
- Competitive supply: how many comparable properties were coming to market in relevant submarkets
- Price per square meter benchmarks by neighborhood and building quality tier

Without this data, each investment decision required a team of analysts to spend days manually browsing property portals, calling brokers, and compiling disconnected data points into Excel models — an approach that was both slow and inconsistent.

The team evaluated three options: hiring dedicated real estate research analysts, subscribing to commercial real estate data services, or using automated data extraction. Commercial services provided good coverage for established markets but had significant gaps in the specific Gulf submarkets the conglomerate was targeting. The in-house analyst option was expensive and slow to deploy. Clymin's managed extraction approach offered coverage precision and speed that neither alternative could match.

## The Approach: Multi-City Property Data Extraction

Clymin's engagement began with a market scoping exercise covering the conglomerate's target investment geographies: Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, Jeddah, and Doha. The research team identified the primary data sources for each city — major real estate portals, developer project sites, and government land registry interfaces where available.

The extraction scope was configured to cover:

**Residential Properties:**
- Apartment listings by size (studio, 1BR, 2BR, 3BR+) across target neighborhoods
- Villa and townhouse listings with full price and specification details
- Off-plan project launches with developer name, project location, pricing, and payment plan details

**Commercial Properties:**
- Office space availability and rental rates by building grade (Grade A, B, C) and district
- Retail space listings including high-street locations, mall concessions, and standalone units
- Warehouse and logistics space for investment opportunities adjacent to the group's retail operations

**Market Trend Data:**
- Price per square meter by neighborhood updated weekly
- New listing volume as a supply indicator
- Average days on market as a demand signal
- Developer completion and handover announcements

Data was delivered as a structured weekly report mapped to the conglomerate's internal market framework, with Excel-compatible exports for the strategy team and API access for the group's new data team that was beginning to build analytical infrastructure.

Clymin established the full extraction pipeline within 72 hours of the scoping call, covering 15+ real estate portals and developer sites across five cities.

### Handling the Gulf Real Estate Data Landscape

The Gulf real estate portal landscape presents specific technical challenges. Major portals including Property Finder, Bayut, and Dubizzle use JavaScript-heavy interfaces with search result pagination that requires headless browser automation. Many developer project sites are single-page applications where listing data loads dynamically.

Clymin's infrastructure handled these technical requirements as standard practice, applying headless browser automation with residential proxy rotation in each target market. This approach produced authentic browsing patterns consistent with genuine UAE, Saudi, and Qatari residential IP addresses, maintaining reliable data access across all target portals.

Arabic-language content normalization was also required for Saudi and Qatari sources where property descriptions appear in Arabic. Clymin's normalization layer extracted numerical fields — prices, sizes, coordinates — accurately regardless of language, and applied transliteration for property names and neighborhood labels to ensure consistent identification across sources.

## The Results: 35% Efficiency Improvement

Four months after deployment, the strategy team assessed outcomes against the team's previous research process.

**Research cycle time dropped from 8-10 days to 5-6 days** per market analysis — a 35% efficiency improvement that enabled the team to evaluate more opportunities in the same time and respond more quickly to time-sensitive investment decisions.

**Coverage expanded from 2 cities to 5 cities** with no additional analyst headcount. Previously, a comprehensive multi-city market analysis required allocating 2-3 analysts for a week per city. With Clymin delivering data for all five cities simultaneously, the team's analytical capacity effectively tripled on cross-market assignments.

**Data consistency improved dramatically.** The previous manual process produced market snapshots that were only as consistent as the individual analyst's methodology. Automated extraction applied identical collection logic across all cities, making city-to-city price comparisons reliable for the first time.

**New listing detection became proactive rather than reactive.** When a significant new development launched in a target submarket, Clymin's monitoring detected it within 24 hours. Previously, the team might learn about a major new supply addition weeks after launch, when it was too late to incorporate it into investment timing decisions.

**The group's first direct real estate acquisition** was supported by Clymin data as part of the due diligence process, with the strategy team using neighborhood price trend data and competitive supply analysis to validate the pricing and timing assumptions in the investment model.

## Key Takeaways for Real Estate Investment Teams

**Systematic data collection is a competitive advantage in real estate.** The Gulf real estate market moves quickly, particularly in high-demand residential and prime commercial segments. Teams with automated market intelligence respond to opportunities and threats faster than those depending on broker relationships and manual research.

**Multi-city coverage requires automation.** Manually maintaining current market intelligence across five cities in three countries is not a realistic research model. Automation is the only way to maintain analytical coverage at geographic scale without proportional increases in headcount.

**Property portal data quality varies by market.** The UAE portals (Property Finder, Bayut) have the most comprehensive and accurate listings in the region. Saudi portals are improving but have more variation in listing quality. Clymin's market-specific extraction configuration accounts for these quality differences, applying appropriate validation and deduplication logic for each source.

**Structured data enables investment models that informal research cannot.** The conglomerate's strategy team built quantitative scoring models for location selection once consistent, structured data was available. These models replaced the informal "best guess" approach that had characterized earlier site selection decisions.
