Evaluating a managed web scraping service requires asking specific questions about accuracy, setup time, compliance, pricing, scalability, and support before signing a contract. The wrong vendor costs more than its fees — inaccurate data produces bad decisions, and unreliable delivery creates operational risk that outweighs the convenience of outsourcing. Clymin, with 12+ years of experience and 750+ completed projects across e-commerce, travel, financial services, and real estate, answers all ten of these questions transparently and in writing before every engagement.

## Why the Evaluation Process Matters

Managed web scraping is a vendor relationship with high ongoing dependency. Once your pricing models, competitive intelligence processes, or investment research workflows are built on data from a specific provider, switching is operationally disruptive. Investing time in rigorous vendor evaluation upfront saves significantly more time and money over the life of the relationship.

The questions below address the dimensions that most commonly become problems in managed scraping relationships. Each question has a right answer and wrong answer — and knowing the difference before signing prevents expensive mistakes.

## Question 1: What Is Your Documented Data Accuracy Rate?

**Why it matters:** Data accuracy is the fundamental quality metric. An 85% accuracy rate sounds high until you realize it means 1 in 6 prices is wrong. For pricing decisions, that error rate generates systematically bad competitive intelligence.

**Right answer:** A specific percentage (99%+ is achievable for structured data), a description of how accuracy is measured, and a commitment to providing accuracy reports with each delivery.

**Red flag:** Vague language like "high accuracy" or "industry-leading quality" without specific numbers. Any provider that cannot quantify their accuracy rate does not have a mature quality assurance process.

**Clymin's answer:** 99%+ accuracy on structured data fields including pricing, availability, and numerical metrics. Daily validation reports covering schema validation, range checks, and anomaly detection are standard with every delivery.

## Question 2: How Long Does Setup Take?

**Why it matters:** Weeks or months of setup delay means months without competitive data while your team waits. Long setup times also indicate that the provider does not have pre-built infrastructure for your target sources.

**Right answer:** 48-72 hours for standard data sources with established site support. Custom or novel sources may take longer, but the provider should give a specific, committed timeline.

**Red flag:** Open-ended timelines like "a few weeks" or "depends on complexity" without a specific commitment. Vagueness on timeline typically means the provider does not have a production-ready infrastructure for your target.

**Clymin's answer:** 48-hour setup for standard e-commerce, travel, financial, and real estate data sources. Custom scope for novel sources, with a committed timeline provided during scoping.

## Question 3: Is Maintenance Included in the Price?

**Why it matters:** Web scrapers break regularly. Target websites update their layouts, change their DOM structure, implement new anti-bot measures, and modify their data presentation. If maintenance is not included, you face either growing costs as the vendor bills for fixes or data outages when scrapers break and no one fixes them.

**Right answer:** Yes, maintenance is fully included in the monthly fee. The provider should be able to describe their maintenance process: how they monitor for failures, how quickly they respond, and how clients are notified of issues.

**Red flag:** Maintenance billed hourly or as separate "professional services." A provider that charges per fix creates a misaligned incentive — they profit from scrapers breaking.

**Clymin's answer:** Full maintenance is included in every engagement. Clymin monitors all active pipelines continuously, detects failures before clients notice them, and resolves issues as a standard operational responsibility.

## Question 4: What Is Your Anti-Bot Handling Approach?

**Why it matters:** Major e-commerce, travel, and financial data sources deploy sophisticated bot detection. Providers without mature anti-bot infrastructure produce inconsistent data with high failure rates on protected targets.

**Right answer:** A description of specific techniques including proxy rotation (residential preferred for consumer-facing sites), headless browser automation for JavaScript-rendered pages, adaptive request patterns, and CAPTCHA handling methods. The provider should explain how they handle new anti-bot measures when targets deploy them.

**Red flag:** Claims of "unlimited scaling" or "guaranteed access" without explaining methodology. Also watch for providers that describe only proxy rotation without mentioning browser rendering — many complex sites require headless browser automation that proxy rotation alone cannot solve.

**Clymin's answer:** Residential and datacenter proxy rotation, Playwright/Puppeteer headless browser automation for JavaScript-rendered targets, adaptive request patterns tuned per target site, and a dedicated infrastructure team monitoring and adapting to anti-bot changes across all active clients.

## Question 5: What Compliance Certifications Do You Hold?

**Why it matters:** Enterprise procurement and compliance teams require documented security certifications before approving data vendors. A vendor without ISO 27001 and SOC 2 is a procurement blocker for many organizations. For regulated industries, compliance documentation is not optional.

**Right answer:** ISO 27001 certification, AICPA SOC 2 Type II compliance, GDPR adherence documentation. The provider should be able to share current certificates, not just claim the certifications.

**Red flag:** Claims of compliance without certification documentation. "We follow GDPR principles" is not the same as demonstrable GDPR adherence with a written processing agreement.

**Clymin's answer:** ISO 27001 certified, AICPA SOC compliant, GDPR adherent. Full documentation available for procurement review, vendor due diligence, and regulatory examination responses.

## Question 6: How Do You Handle Data Normalization and Delivery?

**Why it matters:** Raw scraped data is not the same as usable data. Prices in inconsistent formats, fields in different naming conventions across sources, and unstructured text that needs parsing all require normalization. If the provider delivers raw HTML or inconsistently formatted CSVs, your team handles the normalization work.

**Right answer:** Structured, normalized output in a format specified by the client. The provider should describe their normalization process: how they standardize fields across sources, handle missing values, and validate format consistency.

**Red flag:** "We deliver the raw data and you can process it however you need." This means the provider is in the infrastructure business, not the data business.

**Clymin's answer:** Fully normalized, structured delivery in client-specified formats (JSON, CSV, Parquet, or direct database delivery). Normalization includes currency standardization, field naming consistency across sources, missing value handling, and category taxonomy alignment.

## Question 7: What Are Your SLAs on Data Freshness and Uptime?

**Why it matters:** Data that arrives two days late is stale for pricing decisions. A service with no uptime SLA can fail for days without penalty. SLAs align vendor incentives with your actual operational requirements.

**Right answer:** Specific SLAs on delivery schedule adherence (e.g., data arrives within 2 hours of scheduled delivery time, 99.5% uptime), a defined process for SLA failures (alerts, credits, escalation), and a track record of SLA performance.

**Red flag:** No documented SLAs or SLAs with so many exceptions they provide no meaningful commitment.

**Clymin's answer:** Committed delivery windows with SLA documentation provided in engagement contracts. Clients receive proactive alerts for any delivery delays, and Clymin's operational track record across 750+ projects demonstrates consistent on-schedule delivery.

## Question 8: How Does Pricing Scale with Volume?

**Why it matters:** Many providers offer attractive entry pricing that becomes expensive at scale. Understanding how pricing scales ensures the relationship remains economically sound as data requirements grow.

**Right answer:** Clear explanation of how pricing scales with source count, data volume, and refresh frequency. Linear or sub-linear scaling is fair; exponential scaling creates adoption traps. No surprise add-ons for infrastructure costs that were not in the original proposal.

**Red flag:** Pricing that is competitive at low volumes but scales aggressively once you are dependent on the service. Ask explicitly for the pricing at 2x and 10x your initial scope.

**Clymin's answer:** Scope-based pricing that scales sub-linearly with volume as shared infrastructure benefits reduce per-unit costs. Full pricing transparency from $100 per project, with no separate charges for proxy infrastructure, maintenance, or quality assurance.

## Question 9: Can You Provide Client References?

**Why it matters:** References validate that the provider has actually delivered results at the scale and for the use case you require. Generic testimonials on a website are insufficient; specific contacts who can discuss their experience are the meaningful reference.

**Right answer:** Willingness to provide references in your industry or use case, with specific outcomes they can describe.

**Red flag:** References only through anonymous testimonials, case studies with no verifiable details, or reluctance to connect prospective clients with existing customers.

**Clymin's answer:** Clymin references are available upon request for qualified prospects, including clients in e-commerce, travel, financial services, and real estate. Client outcomes include 20% revenue increases, 99%+ data accuracy at scale, and 25% faster decision-making for financial research teams.

## Question 10: What Happens When Your Service Fails?

**Why it matters:** Every data pipeline fails at some point. The difference between a good vendor and a bad one is how quickly they detect failures, how they communicate them, and how fast they resolve them. A vendor that does not have a documented incident response process will leave you in the dark during the most critical moments.

**Right answer:** A documented incident response process including automated failure detection, proactive client notification within a defined time window, escalation paths for prolonged failures, and post-incident root cause analysis.

**Red flag:** "We will fix it when you report it." Reactive-only incident response means data gaps persist until your team notices them — which, for some datasets, could be days.

**Clymin's answer:** Continuous pipeline monitoring with automated failure detection. Clients receive proactive alerts for any delivery issues within two hours. Resolution SLAs are defined by severity tier, with critical data feeds receiving priority response around the clock.
