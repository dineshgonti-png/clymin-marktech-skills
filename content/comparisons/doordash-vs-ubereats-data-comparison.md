## The Food Delivery Data Landscape in 2026

Food delivery has become one of the most data-intensive sectors in consumer technology. DoorDash, UberEats, Grubhub, and regional platforms collectively process over 60 million orders daily in the US alone, generating vast amounts of publicly visible data about restaurant supply, pricing, promotional activity, and delivery geography.

[Bloomberg Second Measure's 2025 Consumer Spending Report](https://secondmeasure.com/datapoints/food-delivery-industry-overview/) tracks DoorDash at approximately 67% US market share, UberEats at approximately 23%, and Grubhub at roughly 7%, with the remainder distributed across regional platforms. This market concentration makes DoorDash and UberEats the two essential data sources for any comprehensive US food delivery market analysis.

For food tech operators, competitive investors, and market researchers, the data available on these platforms reveals restaurant supply dynamics, pricing strategies, promotional spending, and delivery geography that would otherwise require expensive proprietary research.

India's leading food tech platform uses competitive intelligence from both platforms as part of its global benchmarking program, comparing pricing mechanics, restaurant onboarding strategies, and promotional models against the global market leaders to inform domestic strategy decisions.

## What DoorDash Data Reveals

DoorDash's platform surface area provides several distinct data categories for market analysis.

### Restaurant Supply Coverage

DoorDash maintains the most comprehensive restaurant listing database in the US market. Extractable fields per restaurant include name, address, cuisine categories, dietary labels (vegan, halal, kosher, gluten-free), operating hours, pickup availability, catering availability, group order support, and DashPass participation status.

Restaurant coverage analysis reveals competitive positioning in specific geographic markets. A new food delivery operator entering a city can map the DoorDash restaurant supply in their target zones, identifying cuisine categories and neighborhoods where supply is strong (high competition) versus thin (opportunity).

### Menu Pricing Architecture

DoorDash menus follow a consistent structure: category sections, menu items with names and prices, item customization options (modifiers and add-ons with incremental pricing), and promotional items flagged with badges. Full menu extraction captures this hierarchy in structured form.

Cross-restaurant menu price analysis reveals how different restaurant segments price on the platform. Fast casual chains typically enforce consistent pricing across locations; independent restaurants vary more. Category-level price benchmarking identifies segments where platform pricing pressure is highest.

### Delivery Fees and Service Charges

DoorDash charges delivery fees that vary by restaurant distance, order size, demand level, and DashPass membership status. Non-DashPass delivery fees from specific restaurants, DashPass member fees for the same restaurants, and service fee structures are all extractable from the platform surface.

Delivery fee competitive analysis is particularly relevant for platforms comparing their fee structures to market benchmarks. A platform charging significantly above or below DoorDash's fees in comparable markets faces either a conversion disadvantage (higher fees) or margin pressure (lower fees).

### Promotional Intelligence

DoorDash's promotional landscape includes platform-funded discounts (DoorDash Deals), restaurant-funded promotions, and hybrid arrangements. The visible promotional data — percentage discounts, dollar-off thresholds, free item promotions, bundle deals — signals the level of marketing investment flowing into specific restaurant categories and market segments.

## What UberEats Data Reveals

UberEats provides overlapping but distinct data from DoorDash, with particular strength in urban markets and international geographies.

### Urban Market Depth

UberEats historically developed stronger restaurant networks in major urban markets, where Uber's existing driver supply and brand recognition supported faster food delivery growth. For urban market analysis, UberEats restaurant coverage provides a more complete picture of city center supply, particularly for upscale dining segments.

Ghost kitchen and virtual brand presence is often higher on UberEats in urban markets, reflecting the platform's early embrace of virtual restaurant concepts. Extracting virtual brand listings and their parent kitchen relationships provides competitive intelligence on the ghost kitchen supply landscape.

### International Coverage

UberEats operates in 45+ countries, making it the primary data source for food delivery market analysis outside the US. For food tech companies expanding internationally, or for investors evaluating international food delivery markets, UberEats provides standardized market data across geographies that DoorDash's primarily US-focused footprint cannot.

Clymin extracts UberEats data across North America, Europe, Asia Pacific, Latin America, and the Middle East, supporting international competitive benchmarking for food delivery operators.

### Uber One Pricing Intelligence

UberEats' Uber One subscription program creates a two-tier pricing structure — standard pricing and Uber One member pricing — that is visible in the platform data. Extracting both price points reveals the effective discount level the platform provides to subscription members, which is material for understanding platform economics and competitive positioning against DoorDash's DashPass.

### Restaurant Ratings and Review Velocity

UberEats displays restaurant ratings alongside menu content. Rating trends over time, review velocity, and rating distribution across an operator's restaurant supply provide service quality signals relevant to market researchers and investors evaluating platform health.

## Cross-Platform Price Analysis: The Most Valuable Signal

The most strategically valuable insight from combined DoorDash and UberEats data is cross-platform price comparison for the same restaurants. Many restaurants charge different prices on DoorDash versus UberEats — either because different commission structures force different menu pricing, because promotional arrangements vary by platform, or because the restaurant has negotiated different terms.

Clymin's cross-platform matching identifies the same physical restaurant on both platforms using name, address, and menu overlap, then compares item-level pricing to quantify the cross-platform price differential. For food delivery platforms, this analysis reveals:

- Which restaurants are charging a price premium on your platform relative to the competition
- Where competitors are funding restaurant-level discounts that affect conversion
- Category patterns in cross-platform pricing that reveal commission structure dynamics

For restaurant groups, cross-platform pricing analysis reveals inconsistencies in their own multi-platform pricing strategy that could be undermining conversion on higher-commission platforms.
