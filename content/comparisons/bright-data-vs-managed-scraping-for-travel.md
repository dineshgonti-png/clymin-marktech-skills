## What Bright Data Provides

Bright Data offers proxy infrastructure: a network of 72+ million residential IPs, datacenter proxies, ISP proxies, and a Web Unlocker product that handles CAPTCHA solving and anti-bot navigation automatically. For teams building their own scrapers, Bright Data solves the proxy and anti-bot problem.

The platform does not provide scrapers, extraction logic, data normalization, or competitive intelligence outputs. Bright Data delivers raw proxy access and some tooling for browser automation. Building a functioning hotel rate monitoring program on top of Bright Data requires engineering resources to write and maintain scrapers for each OTA target.

Travel sites are among the most technically complex scraping targets. Booking.com, Expedia, and Agoda all use JavaScript-heavy rendering, session-based pricing that returns different rates in different contexts, aggressive anti-bot detection, and geographic IP filtering that shows different rates based on request origin. Maintaining scrapers for 10+ OTAs against this technical environment is a significant ongoing engineering investment.

## The True Cost of Bright Data for OTA Monitoring

**Proxy costs**: Bright Data residential proxy pricing for OTA-scale extraction (daily rate checks across 10+ OTAs with reasonable depth) typically runs $500–$2,000/month depending on data volume and OTA platform complexity.

**Engineering labor**: Travel site scrapers require more maintenance than any other scraping domain — OTA platforms update their anti-bot infrastructure, page structure, and rate display logic frequently. According to HSMAI (2024), hotels managing in-house OTA monitoring allocate one-third to one-half of a technical staff member's time to infrastructure maintenance.

At $10,000/month fully-loaded engineering cost (partial FTE), a 35% time allocation equals $3,500/month in labor. Add Bright Data infrastructure costs and total monthly cost for a Bright Data-based OTA monitoring program: $4,000–$5,500/month minimum, scaling higher with coverage expansion.
